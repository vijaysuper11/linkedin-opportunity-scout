# Profile Extraction Guide

## 1. Offer map
For every candidate capability, record: capability, evidence level, evidence line (quote or close paraphrase from the profile), and recency.

Evidence levels:
- **strong**: appears in a role description, project, certification, or result. Example: "Led migration of 40 client sites" backs a migration capability.
- **light**: appears only in the Skills list or headline with no supporting detail. May be mentioned briefly and honestly ("I also work with X") but never as the main pitch.
- **none**: absent from the profile. Never offered, even if the post asks for it.

Rank by recency and repetition: skills used in the latest two roles outrank old ones. Treat the headline as the owner's chosen focus, and note when the headline and experience disagree.

## 2. Target demand phrases
Think from the buyer's side. For each strong capability, write 3 to 6 ways a real poster would ask for it:
- Direct asks: "looking for a [role]", "need a [role]", "hiring a [role]", "seeking [capability] help"
- Problem statements: "our [thing] is slow / broken / outdated, anyone who can fix"
- Referral asks: "can anyone recommend a [role]", "know a good [role]?"
- Scope words: contract, freelance, project, part-time, short-term, retainer, consultant, advisor

Add synonyms and adjacent titles used in the owner's field. Add 5 or more negative terms to filter out job seekers and wrong fits (for example "#opentowork", "seeking opportunities", unrelated seniority levels).

## 3. Engagement fit
Infer from the profile (Open to work settings, headline wording, role history), then confirm. Common types: full-time, contract, freelance, part-time, consulting, advisory, mentoring, speaking, partnership, co-founder. Record "wanted", "open to", and "not interested".

## 4. Eligibility
- Location, time zone, languages
- Work-authorisation hints (visa, citizenship) only if the owner states them
- Remote, hybrid, or on-site willingness
Posts that require something the owner cannot meet are skipped, with the reason stated.

## 5. Proof points
Only facts stated in the profile or told by the owner. Store as: claim, source (profile section or owner chat), safe to say publicly (yes, no, ask). If a number or client name is present, mark it `ask` until the owner confirms it can be public.

## 6. Tone
From the About section and any recent posts: average sentence length, formal or casual, use of questions, first-person style, favourite words. Note 5 to 8 bullets plus 3 words the owner would never use.

## 7. Weak spots to avoid leaning on
- Skills with no evidence
- Gaps or overlaps in dates
- Vague claims such as "expert in everything"
- Anything the owner marked private
